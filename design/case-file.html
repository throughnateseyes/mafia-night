case no. 26 · one direction, built from your references

A weekend in Pioneer

The game is a murder case file, and everyone at the cabin is a suspect. You don’t “join a game”, you get booked. Your role comes in a confidential folder. Deaths are reported in the local paper. The vote happens on a detective’s pinboard. When night falls the lights go out and all you can see is the cabin by firelight.

On everyone’s phone

The eight moments of a game, in order.

Your phone, Nate: the remote

You run the whole night from the lead detective’s folder. Nobody else can see it.

The morning paper has editions

Same template, different story depending on what happened overnight. The birthday edition only runs if the person you marked as the birthday girl is the one who dies.

Three ways to letter the title

No more cut-out letters. Each of these has real ink, wobble and depth.

On the laptop or TV

Same desk, wider. The join code is a card taped to the table, and the cabin fills up as people scan in.

Why each choice is there

Two moods, one deskDay screens are paperwork under a lamp. Night screens are the same desk with the lights off and the fire going. The switch between them is the big moment of the game, so it gets the biggest visual change.

Type that sets the sceneTypewriter for the file and the title card, tabloid headlines for deaths, pen scrawl for the detective’s notes. No all-caps labels, no default app fonts.

Texture you can nameManila folders, aged typing paper, torn newsprint, cork, tape, wood grain, black-and-white photos. Each is a real material from your references, used where it would be in real life.

The cabin is aliveThe lobby is a floor plan lit by a fire that flickers and throws shadows. People walk in through the door when they join and shuffle around while they wait.

Made for StephIt’s case no. 26 for her 26th. Her name is on the file, the Pioneer Gazette reports on her friends, and the cake shows up when the campers win.

Suspects you’ll fight overPlayer icons are woodland animals in old police booking photos. Each holds a blank letter board, and the app prints your name on it.

RestraintOne prop per screen carries the idea. Everything else stays quiet so the game is still easy to read in a dark room.
